People called it ₹20 chapri drink, but now it is the official drink of Formula One. And this is not a beverage story. This is a brutal lesson in distribution. Let me tell you.

When you think of Red Bull, you don't think of caffeine, you think of Formula One, speed, extreme sports, adrenaline rush. Red Bull sold a lifestyle. But Sting played a different game. People laughed at it, called it poor man's Red Bull, a roadside energy drink. But Sting did not chase premium perception first, it chased reach. Small shops, railway stalls, college canteens, roadside dhabas, everywhere, and slowly, visibility became familiarity, familiarity became trust, and trust became market share.

This is the D2C lesson. Your customer cannot buy from a brand they cannot see. Branding creates desire, distribution creates dominance. Sting entered India at an affordable price point, built mass availability, became one of India's leading energy drinks, and through F1's partnership, entered Red Bull's own playground.

This is not luck. This is the power of distribution. Save and share this if you are building a D2C brand.
